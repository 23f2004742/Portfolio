Chapter Zero

Not a resume.
A life.

The other page lists what I have built. This one tells you how I got here: born in Brahmapur, moved six times, went through five schools, failed a board exam, published a book, and ended up building a company out of IIT Madras. Twenty two years, told in order.

01
2004 Age 0 Brahmapur, Odisha

It starts in Brahmapur

A coastal town in southern Odisha. Silk city, temple bells, an accent that still comes out when I am tired or angry. I did not choose it and I did not stay long, but it is the only place on this page that has been home twice.

Eight months later we were gone. That is the shortest my life has ever stayed still, and it set the pattern for everything after.

What it gave meA first language and a place to come back to.
02
2005 8 months Surat, Gujarat

The first move, before I could walk

Odisha to Gujarat. Roughly seventeen hundred kilometres, one coast to the other, a completely different language on the shop boards. I was eight months old, so I have no memory of the journey, only of what it made me: a kid who was never precious about location.

I started school here. LKG in Surat, first classroom, first uniform, first set of friends I would eventually leave.

What it gave meMoving is normal. Starting over is a skill, not a punishment.

03
2009 - 2014 Age 5 to 10 Back to Brahmapur

Odia medium, and the basics of being a person

We went back. My foundational schooling happened here, entirely in Odia medium, and so did everything underneath school. How to eat, how to talk, how to argue, how to lose. The unglamorous operating system that every later version of me runs on.

Two things defined those years. One, I was a total Sachin Tendulkar kid, the kind who copies the stance in the living room. Two, I turned out to be unreasonably good at general knowledge and ended up a GK scholar at Odisha state level.

What it gave meCuriosity as a default setting, and a mother tongue I never lost.
04
2014 Age 10 Surat again

Ten years old, third language, second state

Back to Gujarat, and this time into English medium. Imagine switching the language your brain runs school in, at ten, for the second time. That is where Hindi, Gujarati and English all stacked on top of Odia.

Academics went well. Sport went better. Running, chess, kho-kho, cricket, all at once, all seriously. My father has loved cricket since he was a boy, so he put me in a cricket academy. I gave it six honest months and then let that particular dream go. It is one of the first grown-up decisions I remember making.

What it gave meQuitting the right thing early is not failure. It is budgeting.

05
2018 Grade 9 New school

Teenage arrives with a haircut

New school, new everything. This is the chapter of cool hairstyles, funky clothes and a friend group that ran on bicycles. If you were fifteen in an Indian town, you know exactly what that evening looked like.

I also went sport shopping. Tried football, tried volleyball, and got selected at regional level in kho-kho and chess. Two very different games: one is pure sprint and instinct, the other is sitting still and thinking fourteen moves ahead. I wanted both.

What it gave meA body that likes speed and a brain that likes patience. Still negotiating.
06
2019 - 2020 Grade 10 Mostly not in class

Empty notebooks and one obsession

Let me be honest about tenth grade, because the honest version is more useful. I was rarely in class. My notebooks were incomplete. For some subjects there was no notebook at all, because I never bought one.

And then there was maths. Maths I did not need to be told to do. That one subject separated me from the room, carried my boards, and quietly decided the next eight years of my life.

What it gave meWhen one thing genuinely pulls you, protect it. It will pay for the rest.

07
2020 Grade 11 Lockdown

PCM, and then the world closed

I picked Physics, Chemistry, Maths because engineering was the plan. Weeks later Covid-19 shut everything down and school became a rectangle on a screen.

I went all in on an online course and started with a genuine blast: winning competitions, taking home cash prizes, books, and whatever else they were handing out. For a while, remote learning suited me better than the classroom ever had.

The same year a second thing started. I fell into films, series, drama and theatre, and by the end of it there was no title in the Disney catalogue I had not seen. Millions of frames, and a real interest in how stories are put together.

What it gave meProof that I can teach myself, which is the only skill this decade has rewarded.
08
2021 Age 17 FIDE rated boards

Chess, rated, and an honest verdict

I took chess as far as I could take it. Played multiple FIDE rated tournaments, finished near the top in some of them, sat across from people far better than me and learned what that actually feels like.

Here is the part most people leave out of a portfolio: I was not a great player, and I worked that out fast. I did not spend four years pretending otherwise. I kept the game, dropped the fantasy, and years later ended up running the books for a five hundred member chess club instead.

What it gave meCalibration. Knowing the ceiling early is a gift, not a defeat.
09
2021 - 2022 Grade 12 Two headlines, one year

I published a book. I also failed.

In twelfth grade I wrote and published What to Do in Your Teenage, a guide for teenagers on confidence, initiative and actually using those years. It went on Amazon. It did well. I was seventeen.

The same year, I failed my twelfth boards. Badly. I had to sit them again the following year. Both sentences are true and they sit in the same twelve months, which is the most accurate thing on this entire page.

What it gave meA permanent immunity to the idea that one result defines a person.

2022 Age 18 The in-between year

The year I was not supposed to have

While repeating twelfth, I did not sit still. I got into the stock market as a timepass and stayed for the mechanics of it. I made YouTube videos. I got into content creation. Nothing on that list was on any syllabus.

Then I told my father I was not planning to do a bachelor's degree. His answer was one line, and it was not a discussion: you are doing it, whatever it takes. I am glad he did not blink.

What it gave meA market brain, a camera brain, and one very well timed refusal from my dad.
11
2023 Age 19 IIT Madras

From grace marks to the number one campus

I cleared twelfth on grace marks and got into IIT Madras for a BS in Data Science and Applications. Read those two facts in sequence once more, because for a long time I did not believe they could belong to the same person.

The programme is hybrid, which meant I had room to move. I used it. I learnt web development, went looking for an internship, and started paying for my own choices instead of asking for them.

What it gave meThe door. Everything after this, I had to open myself.
12
2023 - 2024 Age 19 to 20 Everywhere

A hobby called leaving

Somewhere in there, travel stopped being a holiday and became a habit. Trains, buses, cheap stays, temples at dawn, forts at noon, beaches at two in the morning.

Thirteen states and union territories so far, from Rajasthan to Tamil Nadu, Delhi to Andhra. It is the cheapest education I have ever bought. You cannot hold a stereotype about a place after you have eaten breakfast in it.

What it gave meThe diversity of India is not a fact I read. It is a thing I have slept in.

2024 Age 20 Paradox, then Chennai

One campus fest changed my address

Paradox, the annual fest, was the first time I properly stood on campus. I walked around for a day and knew, with no evidence and no plan, that I wanted to be here full time.

So I went home, asked my parents for permission to go chase it, and moved to Chennai. Then came the honest bit: months of sad PG life. Bad food, small room, nobody to talk to. It got fixed the way these things always get fixed, by finding people to play cricket with. We liked each other enough to take a shared flat, and that is when the real life started. Parties, late night games, food runs, arguments, all of it.

What it gave meYou do not move for a place. You move for the people you have not met yet.

Interlude

Uncountable friends

Six moves and five schools means I have collected more people than I can name. Not all of them stayed, and that is not a sad sentence. Here is the honest taxonomy.

Still here

The ones who survived every address change. Small group. Load bearing.

No contact left

Numbers that stopped working, accounts that went quiet. I would still recognise them anywhere.

Conscious mind

People I think about on purpose. A conversation I still replay, advice I still use.

Unconscious mind

The ones who shaped a habit or a reflex without either of us noticing at the time.

Reunited

Ran into them years later in a different city and picked up mid sentence.

Never did

Some reunions do not happen. I have made peace with that one slowly.

14
Nov 2024 Age 20 SoApp

Rejected by the process, backed by a professor

I wanted to build something. I had a self-checkout idea using NFC and RFID for organised retail, the kind of thing that removes the queue entirely.

I pitched it through the college channel and got rejected outright. So I went around the process and pitched a professor directly. He said yes. That single conversation turned into backing from IIT Madras, incubation at Nirmaan, and a patent application under review.

What followed was the best kind of chaos: late night grind, games in the workspace, arguments about hardware, and shipping anyway.

What it gave meA no from a form is not a no from a person. Find the person.

2025 Age 21 Courts, then a pitch

The athlete refused to shut up

Even in the middle of a startup, the sportsperson in me kept getting curious. I picked up badminton and basketball from scratch, at twenty one, surrounded by people who had played them for years.

Naturally, I got injured. Days later I had a startup competition at IIT Hyderabad, so I went. Not for a match, not for a campus tour, for the pitch. I limped through that entire trip and it is still one of my favourites.

What it gave meBeginner is a status I am happy to keep re-entering. And an injury is a reason to move slower, not to stay home.

2023 - 2025 The honest ledger

What the startup cost me, in months

Here is my degree, measured properly. First year took sixteen months. Second and third together took twenty four. That is not the brochure pace, and I am not going to dress it up.

The reason is on the chart. In second year I gave eight months almost entirely to the startup. It might read like an excuse. It is not. The academic gain was real but not dramatic, and the thing I learnt in those eight months does not appear on any transcript.

What it gave meEvery choice has an invoice. I can read mine and I would sign it again.

17
2025 Age 21 The hard stop

Told I could not study for a year

One project went wrong and I picked up a disciplinary action from the institute. The stated consequence was blunt: no studying for a year.

It got resolved and I was allowed to continue. I am not going to pretend it was nothing, because it was the single hardest hit in this entire timeline. What it did was simple and permanent: I got serious. Everything after this point on the page is a different person working.

What it gave meThe difference between wanting something and protecting it.

2026 Age 22 Chennai

Where the story currently is

The last few months have been the sharpest of my life. Real depth in machine learning and GenAI, a stack of projects I actually stand behind, research, a patent, events, campus visits, and a national hackathon win in Hyderabad.

It has not all been clean. There was a co-founder removal, which is a short phrase for a long, unpleasant month. Moving to Chennai still came out worth it: new friends on top of the ones I already had, new experiences, better decisions, and a much clearer idea of what I am willing to spend years on.

Where I am pointingAI and ML engineering, built on top of a decade of refusing to stay in one lane.
No one ever stopped me, and no one ever will. Where I fail is where many people start. So I am not stopping. I am starting a new one.
